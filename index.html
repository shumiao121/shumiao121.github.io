Privacy Policy — Kaleap

Effective date: 27 October 2026 App: Kaleap (com.kaleap.camera) Provider: Xi Yang (杨曦), an individual developer ("we", "us") Contact: wandou121@gmail.com

We built Kaleap to be private by default. Shooting, framing guidance and on-device editing all run on your phone. We do not collect your name, email, contacts, or advertising identifiers, and we never sell your data. Some features send a photo — or a piece of text — to an AI service in the cloud; this policy says exactly which features, who receives the data, and how long it is kept.

What the app accesses

Camera. Used to show the live viewfinder and take photos, only while you are using the app. Framing and composition guidance and subject detection run on your phone.

Photos. Photos you take are saved in Kaleap's own album on your phone and, when you choose, to your system photo library. Kaleap keeps a working "original" of each shot on your phone so you can undo edits. Photos you import from your library are copied into Kaleap's album; importing one does not upload it.

Location and compass. Only if you turn on Light (location and compass) or Save where photos were taken (location only, off by default), and only while the app is open — see *Location* below. Never used in the background.

Microphone. Only if you turn on voice. Speech recognition runs on your device — your audio is never uploaded and is not stored.

What you say is turned into text on the phone. If that text is a question for kaka, or a command that needs to find something in the frame ("shoot that key"), the text and a single frame from the viewfinder are sent to our AI service so it can answer or find the object.

The transcribed text is also kept in a log on your device so we can see which phrasings the app failed to understand. That log never leaves your phone — there is no code path that uploads it. You can turn it off in Settings → Privacy → "Help us understand you"; turning it off also deletes what was already kept. The log holds at most the 2,000 most recent lines.

When data leaves your phone

Nothing is sent to the cloud until you agree. The first time you use a cloud feature, Kaleap shows a note naming the companies below and asks you to agree. You can withdraw at any time in Settings → Storage & general → Cloud processing; after that Kaleap runs entirely on your phone and the cloud features stop offering to run.

These are the features that send data, and what each one sends:

Some of these settings stay on until you turn them off. If you pick a master look, that choice is remembered, and from then on every photo you take is uploaded for processing, on this and future launches, until you switch it off. Passer-by removal is remembered the same way, except that a photo goes up only when the phone finds someone other than you in it. You can switch both off at any time from the camera screen.

Photos are sent over an encrypted connection to our own backend (Google Cloud Run, United States), which forwards them to fal.ai to run the AI model. Before a photo is sent, Kaleap removes its EXIF metadata — including GPS location. We do not use your photos to identify you, for advertising, or to build a profile of you.

The Kaleap mark, and AI-generated photos

Photos you save to your photo library or share from Kaleap carry a small Kaleap wordmark in the corner; the copy kept inside the app stays unmarked. Mood transformations that redraw the whole picture — for example the ones that add a person who isn't really there — also carry the standard IPTC "AI-generated" marker in the file, so other apps can recognise them. Please don't strip that marker to pass a generated photo off as real.

Usage analytics

The app records non-personal usage events (which features were used, timings, success or failure, token counts) to our backend on Supabase, so we can see what works and what breaks. They are tied to the anonymous ID described below — never to your name or email — and contain no photos, no location, and no text you said or typed.

Location

Light (sun position). When you turn on the Light feature, Kaleap asks for your approximate location and reads the phone's compass. It uses them to work out where the sun is and the times of sunrise, sunset, golden hour and blue hour, so it can tell you which way to stand. That calculation is pure astronomy and runs entirely on your phone — your location is never sent anywhere for it. Only the last position is kept, rounded to about one kilometre, so the times are ready the next time you open the feature; turning the feature off stops all location and compass updates. Approximate location is enough — Kaleap never asks for precise location.

Where a photo was taken (off by default). If you turn on Settings → Save where photos were taken, Kaleap takes your approximate location while the camera is open and, when a photo is saved to your photo library, gives that place to the library as the photo's location — the same thing the built-in Camera app does — so Memories and the map can group your photos by place. The location goes only into your own photo library: it is not written into the photo file, it is not sent to our backend or to any AI company, and photos you share from inside Kaleap do not carry it. When you share from the Photos app, iOS lets you choose whether to include the location. Kaleap keeps no list of places you have been: the location waits only until the photo is saved, then it is deleted. Turning the setting off, or leaving the camera, stops location updates.

Anonymous identity

Kaleap has no login — you never give us a name, email, or password. When it first connects, the app creates an anonymous account on Supabase. The random ID it gets is used for two things only:

  • as the key for the usage analytics above, and
  • by our backend to cap how much one device can use per day, so a single heavy user cannot use up the shared daily allowance. If the ID is not available, the backend counts by network address instead.

It is not linked to your real identity, not shared with advertisers, and the AI companies never receive it. You can see it in Settings → Storage & general → Anonymous ID. Under the GDPR this ID counts as personal data, which is why it is described here.

Subscriptions

Subscriptions are sold and billed by Apple. We never see your payment details. The app reads your subscription status from the App Store on your phone; the transaction ID stays on your phone and is not sent to our backend.

On-device analysis libraries

Framing and composition guidance run on your device, using Apple's Vision framework on iPhone and Google's ML Kit on Android. These libraries do not upload your images. Google's ML Kit does send Google its own diagnostic and usage metrics from the device (not your images); Google's terms require us to tell you this.

Data we do NOT collect

  • No name, email, password, or social login
  • No contacts, calendar, or social graph
  • No advertising ID; no third-party ad networks; no cross-app tracking
  • No background location tracking

Children

Kaleap is not directed to children under 13 and we do not knowingly collect data from them.

How long data is kept

  • Your photos and originals stay on your phone until you delete them.
  • At fal.ai: we ask fal.ai not to store our requests, and to delete the generated result one hour after it is made. The model companies receive a request only to produce that result; how long they keep it is governed by their agreements with fal.ai, which we cannot see.
  • Our backend's request logs, which include your network (IP) address and the anonymous ID, are kept by Google Cloud for 30 days and then deleted automatically.
  • Usage analytics and the anonymous account on Supabase are kept while Kaleap is in service, or until you ask us to delete them.

Your choices

  • Withdraw cloud processing in Settings → Storage & general → Cloud processing. Everything else keeps working on your phone.
  • Deny or revoke Camera, Microphone or Location permission in your system settings; the related features stop, and everything else keeps working.
  • "Auto-enhance after shooting" runs on your device — leaving it on does not upload anything. What sends photos to the cloud is the features in the table above.
  • Clear stored originals from the album at any time.

Your rights in the EU/EEA and the UK

Kaleap is not currently offered in the EU, the EEA or the UK. If you use it there anyway, the GDPR gives you the rights below. Because Kaleap has no login, here is what each one means in practice:

  • Access (Art. 15) — almost everything Kaleap holds is on your own phone, where you can already see it. What sits on our backend is the anonymous ID and non-personal usage events.
  • Rectification (Art. 16) — write to us about anything inaccurate.
  • Erasure (Art. 17) — deleting the app removes everything on your phone. To delete the backend records tied to your anonymous ID, email us with the ID from Settings → Storage & general → Anonymous ID.
  • Portability (Art. 20) — your photos are ordinary files on your phone; for backend records, ask and we will send JSON.
  • Objection and restriction (Art. 18, 21) — withdraw cloud processing in Settings, or deny a system permission, at any time.
  • Withdraw consent (Art. 7(3)) — at any time in Settings, without affecting processing that happened before.
  • Complain (Art. 77) — to your local data protection authority.

How to exercise them: email wandou121@gmail.com. We answer within one month, free of charge.

Legal bases (Art. 6). On-device shooting and editing: performance of the service you asked for (Art. 6(1)(b)). Sending data to the cloud: your consent (Art. 6(1)(a)), asked before the first upload and withdrawable at any time. Non-personal usage events: our legitimate interest in keeping the app working (Art. 6(1)(f)).
